Understand the customer's operation

Translate ambiguous operational pain into bounded, testable delivery opportunities.

Fictional customer brief

Westhaven Window & Door Co.

An owner-led residential installation business uses spreadsheets, phone calls and untracked handoffs. Staff cannot reliably tell what is blocking a job or who owns the next task.

  • Users: front desk, sales, measurement, vendor, installation, accounting, service, homeowner and management.
  • Primary pain: duplicate work, missing prerequisites and opaque job status.
  • Delivery objective: a single job record and role-aware handoff sequence.

FDE discovery questions

Questions to validate before coding

  1. Which system owns customer, job, measurement and payment truth?
  2. Which handoffs fail most often and who escalates them?
  3. What must be present before procurement, scheduling and closeout?
  4. What exceptions require an owner override and an audit trail?
  5. What business hours, volumes, access restrictions and SLAs apply?

External messaging, lender, payment, e-signature and supplier services are not connected. The customer journey simulator uses browser-local state and is not synchronized with the server-backed operations workspace.

Trade-off: deterministic stage engine

Explicit stage rules and a single job record make handoffs explainable, testable and easier to audit. This sacrifices some flexibility until customer-approved exception paths are defined.

Trade-off: prototype deployment

Python + SQLite enables inspectable workflow behavior with minimal deployment overhead. Managed database storage, backups, stronger identity, observability and tenant isolation are mandatory before real customer use.

Cutover decision gates

  1. Preflight: customer-approved scope, access matrix, integration credentials and data mapping.
  2. UAT: happy path, blocked paths, user roles, exceptions and accessibility.
  3. Go / no-go: data reconciliation, restore test, rollout owner and rollback window.
  4. After release: monitoring, incident routing, issue triage and handover acknowledgment.

These gates are a proposed customer rollout; they have not been performed for a real customer.

Acceptance checklist for a real project

  • Business owner approves end-to-end workflows and exception ownership.
  • Technical owner accepts code, configurations and deployment assets.
  • Support team can diagnose, escalate and restore an interrupted workflow.
  • Security and privacy controls are signed off, with no open release blockers.
  • Runbooks, documentation and training reach each impacted role.
  • Known gaps, open defects and follow-up backlog are recorded explicitly.
Customer sign-off is not claimed for this portfolio demo. These are proposed acceptance criteria for a hypothetical customer engagement.

How to measure a pilot

  1. Define each metric, event timestamp and source-of-truth owner.
  2. Capture pre-rollout baseline over an agreed representative period.
  3. Instrument job state changes, delays, exceptions and assignment events.
  4. Compare post-release cohort results with the same definitions.
  5. Document confounders, residual gaps and recommended next iteration.

No improvement percentages are invented or implied.
